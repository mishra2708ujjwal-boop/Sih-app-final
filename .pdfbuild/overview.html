Start here
Executive Summary

PRANA is an offline-first, on-device health and environment companion for a wrist-worn band. It reads your body and your surroundings, learns what "normal" looks like for you, flags when something is going wrong, and — if you fall or hit the SOS — can send a real SMS, place a real phone call, and share your GPS location, all without any internet connection or server.

In simple terms Imagine a small health guardian that lives entirely inside your phone. A wearable band measures your heart rate, blood-oxygen, skin temperature, motion, and the heat, humidity and air quality around you. PRANA turns those numbers into a single easy-to-read risk score, tells you what to do about it in plain language, and quietly learns your personal baseline over time so it can spot your abnormal — not a textbook average. In an emergency it can raise the alarm by itself. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
Technically A Flutter/Dart (Material 3) Android application, architected offline-first with no backend and no cloud. A single ChangeNotifier controller (PranaHealthController) orchestrates a pipeline: BLE (or a demo generator) → sensor parsing → a frozen 16-feature vector → a personalized on-device inference stage (transparent physiological heuristics plus genuinely on-device unsupervised learning) → an uncapped 0–100 risk score → rule-based recommendations → UI, persistence, and emergency dispatch. Emergency actions are executed by the app's own native Android MethodChannels (no third-party plugins).

What is genuinely notable here

  • Truly offline. Every core feature works in airplane mode; the only outbound path is an emergency SMS/phone call over the cellular network — not the internet.
  • Real per-person learning on the device. A running-statistics baseline (Welford + Mahalanobis distance) and a small autoencoder neural network are trained on the phone itself from your own calm readings — no pre-baked model, no server training.
  • Honest, transparent risk. The domain risks (heat, breathing, cardiovascular, fatigue) come from hand-tuned physiological formulas you can read and defend line-by-line — not an opaque black box.
  • Emergency response that actually fires. On a detected fall (after a cancelable countdown) or a manual SOS, the app dispatches a real SMS with identity + vitals + a Google-Maps location link, and places a real call, through native Android APIs.
Be honest about this on stage The shipped TensorFlow-Lite model files are not used at runtime (the live path is the personalized service described above). There is no encryption at rest yet (the encrypted-database stack is scaffolded but inactive). There are no accuracy/precision/recall numbers, and the displayed model confidence is a fixed placeholder. The release build is currently debug-signed. Section 25 lists every such limitation, and Section 33 gives you the exact "things I must not claim" list. Owning these honestly will impress judges more than hiding them.

How the pieces fit
Project Architecture
In simple terms Think of the app as four stacked layers. At the top is what you see (the screens). Under that sits one brain object that holds all the current numbers and tells the screens when to refresh. The brain calls a set of specialist helpers (one learns your baseline, one scores risk, one writes advice, one watches for falls, one handles emergencies). At the bottom sits where the data comes from and goes (the Bluetooth band or a demo generator, two small on-device stores, and a thin native bridge to Android).
Technically A layered, single-controller architecture. PranaHealthController (a ChangeNotifier) is the composition root and single source of truth; it owns every service and is provided once at the top of the widget tree. Screens subscribe with context.watch / context.read. Two auxiliary providers expose the raw vitals store (read-only, for CSV export) and a live ML-personalization status notifier. Two ValueNotifiers inside the controller (a 10 Hz motion trace and the ML status) push high-frequency updates without rebuilding the whole tree.

From tap to insight
Complete Application Flow
In simple terms When you open the app it sets up its storage and its learning model, shows a short animated splash, then either walks a first-time user through onboarding or drops a returning user straight onto the home screen. From then on, every couple of seconds a fresh reading arrives, the brain runs it through the pipeline, and the screens update.

The per-reading pipeline

Once the shell is up, a sensor stream (real BLE, or the demo generator in Demo mode) delivers a reading roughly every 2 seconds. For each reading the controller runs:

  1. Parse the raw packet into typed readings (health, motion, environment).
  2. Update baseline — feed calm readings into the running "normal" model.
  3. Extract features — build the frozen 16-number feature vector.
  4. Infer — run the personalized inference stage → per-domain risks + an overall anomaly signal.
  5. Score risk — combine into an uncapped 0–100 risk value + level.
  6. Recommend — pick specific advice via the rule cascade.
  7. Notify — call notifyListeners() so subscribed screens repaint.
  8. Persist — roll the day's numbers into a DailySummary; store calm samples for learning.
  9. Watch for falls — the motion stream feeds fall detection independently; a fall arms the emergency countdown.
Important default On a fresh install the sensor source is real BLE (demo mode defaults off). So with no band paired and Demo mode not enabled, the screens legitimately show "no live data yet." For a demo, either pair a band or turn on Demo mode first — otherwise the home screen is intentionally blank.

Where's the server?
"Backend" Explanation
There is no backend — and that's the point PRANA has no server, no cloud, no REST/GraphQL API, no database server, and no authentication service. Searched for and not found in the current codebase. This is a deliberate offline-first design, not a missing piece.
In simple terms Everything a "backend" would normally do — store data, run the analysis, make decisions — happens right on the phone. The phone is the backend. That's why it works with no signal, in a flood or a remote village, and why your health data never leaves the device.
Technically What would be "backend responsibilities" are implemented as an on-device service layer coordinated by PranaHealthController: ingestion (sensor sources), transformation (feature processor), inference (personalized ML service), decision (risk + recommendation engines), persistence (SharedPreferences + sembast), and side-effects (native emergency/file channels). There is no network client in the app at all for its own data.

Where data lives
Database Deep Dive
In simple terms There are two little storage boxes on the phone. The first (SharedPreferences) is a simple labelled key-value box — it holds your profile, your learned baseline, your settings, and up to 90 daily summaries. The second (sembast) is a tiny on-device database that holds the raw "calm" readings the learning model trains on, plus the model itself. Both are plain and local; nothing is encrypted yet.

① SharedPreferences

Via local_storage_service.dart. Simple key→value.

  • User profile (name, age, sex, conditions, contact)
  • Learned baseline (normal HR/SpO₂/temp, readings learned)
  • App settings + onboarding-complete flag
  • DailySummary × up to 90 (retention 90 days)

No raw minute-by-minute history is kept here — only daily rollups.

② sembast (prana_ml.db)

Via vitals_sample_store.dart. Pure-Dart NoSQL.

  • Recorded calm feature vectors (16 numbers + timestamp) — the ML training data
  • The persisted PersonalModel (running stats) & autoencoder weights

Read-only copy exposed to the CSV export screen via a Provider.

Say this, not that ✔ "Data is stored locally in SharedPreferences and a sembast NoSQL database."   ✘ "We use an encrypted SQLCipher database." (The SQLCipher stack is present in pubspec.yaml but not wired in; storage is currently plaintext — see §11 & §25.)

ML / AI Deep Dive

This is the section judges probe hardest, so it is the section you must know cold. The golden rule: describe each layer as exactly what it is — real learning where it is real, transparent heuristics where they are heuristics, and dormant where a file ships but never runs.

What actually runs at inference time (the live path)

Technically — the wiring, verified At startup, main.dart constructs a PersonalizedInferenceService that wraps a MockInferenceService, and injects it into the controller via the customMlService parameter. The controller uses customMlService ?? MockInferenceService() — so with the injection present, PersonalizedInferenceService is the live inference engine. For each 16-feature vector it produces: (1) the five domain probabilities straight from the heuristic MockInferenceService (unchanged, transparent), and (2) a personalized overall-anomaly signal that blends the Mahalanobis distance from the PersonalModel and the autoencoder's reconstruction error, with hard physiological floors so a genuinely dangerous reading can't be "learned away" as normal.

The 16-feature vector (frozen schema v1.1.0)

Every model consumes the same fixed vector, defined once in feature_schema.dart and computed in feature_processor.dart. Freezing the schema means an exported CSV can be de-normalized back to real units exactly — the export is self-verifying. It carries absolute vitals (HR, SpO₂, temp), baseline-relative deviations (e.g. HR % deviation), an HR trend (an OLS slope from hr_trend_calculator.dart), environmental terms (heat index, AQI, humidity) and a categorical activity level.

The three things you must NOT claim about the ML ✘ "It uses a trained neural network for the risk score." → The risk comes from heuristics; the trained autoencoder contributes only a personalized anomaly signal, and the shipped .tflite is unused.   ✘ "It's 90-something-percent accurate." → There are no accuracy/precision/recall metrics; the on-screen confidence (~0.92) is a fixed placeholder.   ✘ "It diagnoses conditions." → It is a wellness/early-warning aid, explicitly not a diagnostic device (the PDF report says so).
The one-paragraph honest ML pitch (memorize this) "PRANA does real machine learning on the device: it learns each person's normal with running statistics and a small autoencoder trained on the phone itself, so it can flag what's unusual for you — completely offline. On top of that, the specific risk scores come from transparent, physiology-based formulas we can explain line by line, rather than an opaque black box. We also ship a separately-trained TensorFlow-Lite model, but I'll be upfront: it isn't wired into the live path yet, and we don't claim accuracy numbers we haven't measured."

If AI came from a service
AI-via-API Analysis
Not found in the current codebase PRANA calls no external AI or ML API. There is no OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Hugging Face, or any cloud-inference client; no API keys, no model endpoints, no HTTP calls for inference. All intelligence runs on-device (see §9).
Why this is a strength, not a gap Because nothing is sent to a third-party AI service, PRANA keeps working with no internet, incurs no per-request cost, adds no network latency, and never exposes health data to an outside provider. For a disaster/rural-resilience product, "no API dependency" is a feature to lead with, not apologise for.
If you were asked to add one later The clean insertion point is the MlInferenceService abstraction that PersonalizedInferenceService and MockInferenceService already implement — a future cloud service could implement the same interface and be swapped in behind a connectivity check, without touching the controller. Today, that is design headroom, not a shipped feature.

Does it stay smooth
Performance
In simple terms The heavy work (learning the model) is pushed off the screen's thread so the UI never stutters, screens keep their state when you switch tabs, and the app only keeps a short rolling window of recent points in memory rather than everything.
  • Isolate for training. Autoencoder training runs in a Flutter compute() isolate — off the UI thread, so scrolling stays smooth during learning.
  • Targeted repaints. Two ValueNotifiers (the 10 Hz motion trace and ML status) update only their widgets via ValueListenableBuilder, avoiding full-tree rebuilds.
  • State preservation. The five main tabs live in an IndexedStack, so switching tabs doesn't rebuild or lose scroll position.
  • Bounded memory. In-memory history buffers are capped (roughly 50–150 recent points); only daily rollups persist long-term.
  • Light inference. The live inference is small-matrix arithmetic + a tiny neural net — cheap enough to run every ~2 s tick.
Not measured There are no formal benchmarks (frame timings, memory profiles, battery draw) in the repo. Claims above are architectural, not measured — say "designed for" rather than citing numbers you don't have.

Can it grow
Scalability
In simple terms Because there's no server, PRANA "scales" trivially in one sense — every phone is fully self-contained, so a million users cost you no servers. What it does not do today is combine data across users or give an authority a fleet-wide view.

Scales naturally

  • Zero server cost — compute is on each device
  • No shared bottleneck, no rate limits
  • Works the same for 1 or 1,000,000 users
  • Privacy scales with it (nothing centralized)

Doesn't scale (by design, today)

  • No cross-user aggregation / population dashboards
  • No multi-device sync per user
  • No central fleet management for authorities
  • Model learns per-device only (no federated pooling)
Growth path Adding an optional sync/aggregation tier later doesn't require re-architecting: the controller already produces clean DailySummary objects and the export layer already serializes readings — those are natural upload units for a future opt-in backend (§26).

Why it's built this way
Technical Decision Log
  1. Offline-first, on-device. The product exists for moments with no connectivity; privacy is a bonus. Everything else follows from this.
  2. Transparent risk over black-box. Physiology-based formulas can be explained and defended; important for a health-adjacent tool without clinical validation.
  3. Real per-person learning. Running-stats baseline + on-device autoencoder so "abnormal" means "abnormal for you," not a generic threshold.
  4. Uncapped 0–100 risk. An earlier demo cap was removed so the score reflects the true computed severity, not a flattering ceiling.
  5. Frozen feature schema (v1.1.0). Guarantees reproducibility and lets the CSV export be de-normalized back to real units exactly.
  6. Own native channels, no new plugins. Deliberate response to build-toolchain fragility; keeps the native footprint auditable.
  7. Two simple stores now, encryption later. Ship a working prototype; leave the encrypted-DB scaffolding in place as a clearly-marked next step.

The 60-Second Explanation

Learn this almost word-for-word. It's your opener when a judge says "so, what is it?" — one calm minute, no jargon.

"PRANA is a personal early-warning system for your health and your surroundings — and it works with no internet at all.

You wear a small band; it streams your heart rate, oxygen, temperature and the air and heat around you to the phone over Bluetooth. On the phone — not on any server — PRANA learns what normal looks like for you, and flags when something drifts toward dangerous. It turns that into a single risk score with a plain-language reason, so you actually understand it.

And if something goes badly wrong — a fall, or you hit SOS — it acts on its own: it texts a trusted contact your vitals and a map link, and places a call. No cloud, no account, nothing leaves your phone unless it's an emergency.
